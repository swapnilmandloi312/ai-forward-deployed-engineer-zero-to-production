# 12 — Portfolio and Interview Loop

## Outcome

Present one project as evidence of judgment across discovery, engineering, operations, and customer value.

## Build the case study

Structure the public story around decisions, not a feature list:

1. **Reality:** user, workflow, baseline, constraints, and stakes.
2. **Narrow win:** why this first slice was chosen.
3. **Architecture:** system boundary, data flow, permissions, and alternatives.
4. **Evidence:** evaluation results, reliability, latency, cost, and safety.
5. **Operations:** alerts, incident, rollback, and handoff.
6. **Outcome:** measured change, assumptions, limitations, and next decision.

Use synthetic or licensed data. Blur is not sufficient anonymization when context can identify a customer.

## Interview drills

- Discover an ambiguous AI request in 20 minutes.
- Design a multi-tenant document or workflow system on a whiteboard.
- Debug a quality drop where model, retrieval, tool, and data changes overlap.
- Explain a security incident and immediate mitigation to an executive.
- Defend buy/build, model, automation, and infrastructure decisions.
- Handle “why not fully autonomous?” using risk and outcome reasoning.
- Convert a successful pilot into an adoption and expansion plan.

## Evidence

- Public case study and architecture diagram
- 3–5 minute product demo including one failure path
- Evaluation report and sanitized incident review
- Resume bullets with scope, action, evidence, and outcome
- A decision log containing rejected options
- Practice interview feedback and improvement list

## Proof gate

You can deliver the 10-minute story without jargon, then spend 30 minutes answering technical and customer questions from evidence. You label targets, estimates, and measured outcomes correctly and state what you would change next.
